Auto Translate

Read every website in your language, except the languages you already read. Reply in theirs with one click.

Getting started

  • After installing, the settings page asks two things: the language to translate websites to, and the languages you also read.
  • From then on, pages in other languages translate by themselves as you scroll. The toolbar icon shows EN (or your language) when a page is translated.
  • Open settings any time from the toolbar menu's gear icon.

Everyday use

See the original

Hold ⌥ Option (Alt on Windows) and hover any paragraph to see the original text. To switch the whole page back, click the toolbar icon and choose Show original.

Reply in the site's language

On a foreign site, type in your own language. A small round button appears inside the text box; click it or press ⌥⇧T (Alt+Shift+T) to convert your text to the site's language. Select part of the text first to convert only that part. ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) undoes it.

Skip a site

Click the toolbar icon and turn on Never translate this site. Manage the list in settings.

Troubleshooting

A page isn't translated

Pages already open when you installed the extension need a reload. The toolbar menu also tells you why a page was left alone (for example, it's in one of your languages) and offers Translate anyway.

The toolbar shows a red “!”

Google Translate couldn't be reached, often because of a network problem or too many requests. It retries by itself. If it keeps happening, add your own Google Cloud Translation API key in settings under Translation service.

A text box doesn't change

A few editors (for example Google Docs) don't accept outside changes. The button tells you when that happens; your text is left untouched.
